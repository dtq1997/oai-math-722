Carleson Schrödinger 收敛的 Sobolev 端点

The Sobolev endpoint in Carleson's Schrödinger convergence problem

OpenAI · 2026-10-062 篇论文未见公开 Lean 代码

证明了对每个 f∈Hn/(2(n+1))(Rn)f\in H^{n/(2(n+1))}(\mathbb R^n)f∈Hn/(2(n+1))(Rn) 和每个维数 n≥2n\ge2n≥2，当 t↓0t\downarrow0t↓0 时，eitΔf→fe^{it\Delta}f\to feitΔf→f 几乎处处成立。

这达到了 Carleson 的 Schrödinger 收敛问题中最优 Sobolev 指数的等号情形，包括平面端点 H1/3H^{1/3}H1/3。

英文原文

Proves almost-everywhere convergence eitΔf→fe^{it\Delta}f\to feitΔf→f as t↓0t\downarrow0t↓0 for every f∈Hn/(2(n+1))(Rn)f\in H^{n/(2(n+1))}(\mathbb R^n)f∈Hn/(2(n+1))(Rn) and every dimension n≥2n\ge2n≥2.

This attains the sharp Sobolev equality case of Carleson's Schrödinger convergence problem, including the planar endpoint H1/3H^{1/3}H1/3.

  1. 平面 Schrödinger 方程的端点收敛

    Endpoint convergence for the planar Schrodinger equation

    摘要

    我们解决了 Carleson 收敛问题的平面 Sobolev 端点情形：对于属于 H1/3(R2)H^{1/3}(\mathbb R^2)H1/3(R2) 的初始数据，当 t↓0t\downarrow0t↓0 时，Schrödinger 演化几乎处处收敛到初始数据。

    该演化通过先取高斯正则化极限来定义；在同一个满测度空间点集上，对每个 0<t<10\lt t\lt 10<t<1，该极限都存在，且所得演化在 t = 0 处连续。

    英文原文

    We resolve the planar Sobolev endpoint of Carleson's convergence problem: for initial data in H1/3(R2)H^{1/3}(\mathbb R^2)H1/3(R2), the Schrödinger evolution converges almost everywhere to the initial data as t↓0t\downarrow0t↓0.

    The evolution is defined by taking the Gaussian regularization limit first; on one full-measure spatial set, this limit exists for every 0<t<10\lt t\lt 10<t<1, and the resulting evolution is continuous at t = 0.

  2. 高维 Schrödinger 方程的端点逐点收敛

    Endpoint pointwise convergence for the Schrodinger equation in higher dimensions

    摘要

    我们解决了每个维数 n ≥ 3 中 Carleson 逐点收敛问题的 Sobolev 端点情形。

    对于属于 Hn/(2(n+1))(Rn)H^{n/(2(n+1))}(\mathbb R^n)Hn/(2(n+1))(Rn) 的初始数据，当 t↓0t\downarrow0t↓0 时，自由 Schrödinger 演化几乎处处收敛到初始数据。

    该演化通过在同一个满测度空间点集上、对区间 0<t<10\lt t\lt 10<t<1 一致地去掉高斯正则化来定义。

    英文原文

    We resolve the Sobolev endpoint of Carleson's pointwise convergence problem in every dimension n ≥ 3.

    For initial data in Hn/(2(n+1))(Rn)H^{n/(2(n+1))}(\mathbb R^n)Hn/(2(n+1))(Rn), the free Schrödinger evolution converges almost everywhere to the initial data as t↓0t\downarrow0t↓0.

    The evolution is defined by removing Gaussian regularization on one full-measure spatial set, uniformly over the interval 0<t<10\lt t\lt 10<t<1.
