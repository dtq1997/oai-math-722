单连通平面区域的热点猜想

The hot spots conjecture for simply connected planar domains

OpenAI · 2026-10-061 篇论文Lean 代码已公开

证明了 Burdzy 的单连通热点猜想的严格形式。

在每个光滑有界单连通平面域上，对应于第一个正 Neumann 特征值的每个非零特征函数都没有内部临界点，因此所有整体极值都在边界上取得。

允许特征值具有重数。

英文原文

Proves a strict form of Burdzy's simply connected hot spots conjecture.

On every smooth bounded simply connected planar domain, each nonzero eigenfunction for the first positive Neumann eigenvalue has no interior critical point, so all global extrema lie on the boundary.

Eigenvalue multiplicity is allowed.

OpenAI · overview.tex

论文

  1. 光滑单连通平面域上的严格热点与内部临界点的不存在性

    Strict hot spots and absence of interior critical points on smooth simply connected planar domains

    摘要

    我们证明了光滑有界单连通平面域的严格热点猜想。

    更确切地说，第一个正 Neumann 特征值对应的每个非零特征函数在内部的梯度都不消失，因此它的所有全局极大值点和极小值点都位于边界上。

    即使该特征值是多重的，这一结论也成立。

    英文原文

    We prove the strict hot spots conjecture for smooth bounded simply connected planar domains.

    More precisely, every nonzero eigenfunction for the first positive Neumann eigenvalue has nonvanishing gradient in the interior, so all its global maxima and minima lie on the boundary.

    This holds even when the eigenvalue is multiple.

# The hot spots conjecture for simply connected planar domains

The following describes the scope of the Lean formalization related to the following accompanying paper(s):

- [Strict hot spots and absence of interior critical points on smooth simply connected planar domains](../../preprints/Strict-hot-spots-and-absence-of-interior-critical-points-on-smooth-simply-connected-planar-domains-September-24-2026/main.pdf)

## Scope

The strict hot-spots conjecture asks whether extrema of a first nonconstant Neumann eigenfunction occur only on the boundary. The formalization proves the stronger interior statement on every nonempty smooth bounded simply connected planar domain: every nonzero eigenfunction in the first positive Neumann eigenspace has nonvanishing gradient throughout the interior. Consequently all global maxima and minima lie on the boundary. The conclusion applies to every eigenfunction even when the eigenvalue is multiple.
