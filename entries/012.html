相邻整数最大素因子的独立性

Independent largest prime factors of consecutive integers

OpenAI · 2026-10-061 篇论文Lean 代码已公开

解决了 Erdős–Pomerance 联合 Dickman 猜想：在普通自然密度意义下，nnn 与 n+1n+1n+1 的最大素因子的对数大小渐近独立。

特别地，满足 P+(n)<P+(n+1)P^+(n)<P^+(n+1)P+(n)<P+(n+1) 的整数的密度为 1/21/21/2。

英文原文

Resolves the Erdős–Pomerance joint Dickman conjecture: the logarithmic sizes of the largest prime factors of nnn and n+1n+1n+1 are asymptotically independent in ordinary natural density.

In particular, the integers satisfying P+(n)<P+(n+1)P^+(n)<P^+(n+1)P+(n)<P+(n+1) have density 1/21/21/2.

论文

  1. 相邻整数的联合 Dickman 分布律

    The joint Dickman law for consecutive integers

    摘要

    设 P+(n)P^+(n)P+(n) 表示 n 的最大素因子。

    我们证明，log⁡P+(n)/log⁡n\log P^+(n)/\log nlogP+(n)/logn 和 log⁡P+(n+1)/log⁡n\log P^+(n+1)/\log nlogP+(n+1)/logn 在普通自然密度意义下渐近独立，且边缘分布为 Dickman 分布。

    这肯定地解决了 Erdős–Pomerance 联合 Dickman 猜想，并推出次序关系 P+(n)<P+(n+1)P^+(n)\lt P^+(n+1)P+(n)<P+(n+1) 的自然密度为 1/2。

    英文原文

    Let P+(n)P^+(n)P+(n) denote the largest prime factor of n.

    We prove that log⁡P+(n)/log⁡n\log P^+(n)/\log nlogP+(n)/logn and log⁡P+(n+1)/log⁡n\log P^+(n+1)/\log nlogP+(n+1)/logn are asymptotically independent in ordinary natural density, with Dickman marginals.

    This resolves the Erdős–Pomerance joint Dickman conjecture positively and implies that the ordering P+(n)<P+(n+1)P^+(n)\lt P^+(n+1)P+(n)<P+(n+1) has natural density 1/2.

# Independent largest prime factors of consecutive integers

The following describes the scope of the Lean formalization related to the following accompanying paper(s):

- [The joint Dickman law for consecutive integers](../../preprints/The-joint-Dickman-law-for-consecutive-integers-September-24-2026/paper.pdf)

## Scope

Let $P^+(n)$ be the largest prime factor of $n$. The formalization proves the joint Dickman law in ordinary natural density: for every $0<a,b<1$, the density of integers satisfying $P^+(n)\le n^a$ and $P^+(n+1)\le n^b$ tends to $\rho(1/a)\rho(1/b)$, where $\rho$ is the Dickman function.

It also proves that each of the orderings $P^+(n)<P^+(n+1)$ and $P^+(n+1)<P^+(n)$ has natural density $1/2$.
